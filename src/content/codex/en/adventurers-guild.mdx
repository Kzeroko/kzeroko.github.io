The Guild does not treat adventuring as a romantic profession. It accepts that some people will walk into danger regardless, and makes sure those people at least have a name, a contract, and someone waiting for them.

## What it claims

The seven lamps on the Guild crest stand for **registration, payment, evidence, rescue, return, arbitration and record**. Most branch halls carry the same line carved above the desk:

> Those who do not return are still owed a settlement.

That promise is what separates the Guild from a private mercenary company.

It calls itself politically neutral, but only on professional rules: it will not decide for a client whether a war is just, and it reserves the right to refuse commissions that amount to killing civilians, recapturing escaped slaves, or looting a lawful settlement under the language of a subjugation.

## Your relationship with it

The Guild is the first system faction you deal with and the longest-running one. Your [adventurer rank](/en/wiki/leveling/) is its registry. It also runs risk grading on stable entrances, return-point timing, and the phase certification that decides whether loot can leave a shattered space at all.

## The contradiction at its centre

Rules written to protect adventurers are equally good at letting a local authority outsource risk and responsibility. A commission that is impeccably documented is sometimes impeccably documented precisely so that nobody has to show up in person.
