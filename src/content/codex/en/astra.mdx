Anyone in Urdas can look up and see the Remote Sky Garden — that small white island floating above the clouds, its lights never going out. For centuries people have called whatever lives there the Azure Moon Goddess, and prayed to her for fair weather, safe roads and a little luck.

Anyone who actually meets her finds the legend was slightly off. She panics when tea is too hot. She files pastries travellers hand her as formal observation data. When you thank her she suddenly has nowhere to look.

She really does watch over this world. It is just that her way of watching is very plain: cross-checking regional weather, listening to what the wind couriers bring back, carefully noting down what bread costs in Lucentrystel today. Prices, she says, tell her earlier than any mana curve that somewhere, people have stopped being alright.

## How she got here

A long time ago she came to Urdas with an observation team. The assignment was to record this world and leave at the appropriate time.

Then the catastrophe happened. She did not leave.

She has lived in this Garden for nine hundred and eighty-seven years since. Colleagues left, or stopped coming back. The empty rooms and mismatched workbenches are still exactly as they were. Alone, she keeps watch on the Star Tower signals, answers border alarms, waters the greenhouse, and writes down every single day.

From 972 ST the network began drifting in ways no natural mana tide accounted for. She observed it for fifteen years. Then she started a device that had been switched off for centuries, and invited a Stranger into this world.

It was the first time she had ever asked anyone for help.
