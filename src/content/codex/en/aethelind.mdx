The Moonshadow forest has never welcomed outsiders, and for centuries the Conclave's answer has been that shutting the doors is what keeps everyone safe. Aethelind is one of the few who does not accept that answer — when the World Tree went wrong, she did not send an envoy. She put a bow on her back and walked to the outer ring.

Her judgement is alarmingly fast. Which path is passable, how many minutes are left, who will be holding a promise eighty years from now — she has the number ready. Spend time around her and you notice the precision is not coldness. It is how she shows she cares: your withdrawal route is in her arithmetic too.

She draws a bow beautifully. Compliment her on it and she will almost certainly correct your bow hand first, and only then say, quietly, "…thank you."

## How she got here

She and her twin sister Lyra were born in a small grove-hold on the forest's edge and adopted into the royal main line as children. The elders still question their standing because of it.

In 859 ST the forest suffered what is now called the White Crown Upheaval, and the previous queen died. That same year the Three Seats raised Aethelind — then 159 years old — to the throne. She has spent the hundred and twenty-eight years since proving she deserves the crown.

Very little of her reign is the stuff of legend: revising gathering permits, rerouting spirit migrations, arbitrating disputes over cross-generational promises. But because she was willing to work through them one at a time, the younger elves have, for the first time, a way to go and study outside.

In 987 ST the outer ring began to change. The Conclave wanted to blame outsiders. She refused, and went to look for herself.
