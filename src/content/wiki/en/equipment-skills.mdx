A piece of equipment — usually a weapon; armour is in development — carries **two or four fixed skill slots**. The count is part of the item and cannot be expanded, so a weapon's slot layout is more or less its identity.

<SkillSlots />

## How to read a layout

The letters tell you how the item wants to be played before you read a single number:

- **More A** — nothing. There is never more than one Active.
- **More P** — the item works on its own. Good for a first weapon or a backup.
- **More L** — the item needs the rest of your kit. High ceiling, low floor.

An `ALLL` weapon in an unbuilt loadout is worse than a plain `APPP` one; the same weapon in a finished build can be the best thing you own.

<Callout type="tip" title="Read the slots before the stats">
Two weapons with identical damage and opposite layouts belong in completely different builds. The stat line tells you how hard it hits; the slot line tells you whether it fits.
</Callout>

## Interaction with sockets

Skill slots and [gem sockets](/en/wiki/gems-and-sockets/) are separate systems on the same item:

| | Skill slots | Gem sockets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Count | Fixed by the item | Set by its socket profile |
| Changeable | No | Yes — insert, extract, reroll, reshape |
| Unlocked by | — | Equipment level, seals |

A `SKILL`-kind gem socket can host a passive proc or a manually activated equipment skill of its own. It does not consume one of the item's A/P/L slots — it is an additional line, gated by the socket rules instead.
