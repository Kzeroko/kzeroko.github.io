Every creature in KTM2 carries one or more **defence elements**. Incoming damage is compared against each of them, and the result multiplies your damage before armour is applied.

## The multiplier

| Match | Multiplier |
| --- | --- |
| Damage type the element is weak to | **+25%** |
| Damage type the element resists | **−25%** |
| Anything else | ±0% |

### Stacking across multiple elements

When a creature has several elements and your damage type is effective against more than two of them, each layer beyond the second adds another **10%**.

<Callout type="note" title="Worked example">
A creature carrying Fire, Lightning and Earth, hit by a damage type that is effective against all three:

`125% + (2 × 10%) = 145%`

The same rule mirrors on the penalty side:

`75% − (2 × 10%) = 55%`
</Callout>

This is the reason a single "best" weapon does not exist. Against a triple-element enemy the difference between the right and wrong damage type is roughly **2.6×**.

## Damage types vs defence elements

Not every damage type is something a creature can *be*. Thirteen elements can be worn as defences; several more exist only as damage:

**Defence elements** — Normal, Dragon, Shard, Arcane, Fire, Water, Frost, Wind, Earth, Soul, Astral, Light, Dark

**Damage-only types** — Lightning, Blast, Flame, Melee, Execute, Healing, Wither, Thorns, Trident

Healing appearing as a damage type is not a bug: against Soul and Dark, a heal is a weapon.

## Building around it

- **Elemental vulnerabilities** ([Attributes & effects](/en/wiki/attributes-and-effects/)) let you *create* a weakness rather than wait for one. Apply `ignite`, then hit with Wind.
- The nested spell power attributes (`aether_power` → `dys_aether` → `elemental_power`) exist so a hybrid build can carry several schools without a separate stat line for each.
- Element gems ([Gems & sockets](/en/wiki/gems-and-sockets/)) add a proc from a configured magic school, which is the cheapest way to cover a school your weapon does not have.
